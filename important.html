1. Artificial Intelligence in Classroom

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is no longer a futuristic concept but a reality that is slowly reshaping our classrooms. In the context of a developing nation, the integration of AI in education offers both groundbreaking opportunities and significant challenges. For a student at the HSC level, AI tools like ChatGPT or grammar checkers serve as personalized tutors, helping to explain complex theories in physics or correcting English composition errors instantly. This technology can democratize education, providing high-quality resources to students who may not have access to expensive coaching centers. However, there is a flip side to this advancement. Over-reliance on AI can severely hamper critical thinking and creativity. If we simply copy-paste answers generated by algorithms, we fail to learn the core concepts required for our exams and future careers. Furthermore, the digital divide remains a pressing issue; while some urban colleges may adapt quickly, rural institutions might lag behind, widening the educational gap. Therefore, AI should be viewed as an assistive tool to enhance human intelligence, not replace it. Teachers must guide us on ethical usage, ensuring that we use technology to brainstorm and refine our ideas rather than using it as a shortcut to bypass hard work.

2. Bullying/ Cyber-bullying

Bullying has evolved from playground intimidation to a pervasive digital threat known as cyberbullying, deeply affecting the mental health of teenagers. In our society, traditional bullying often occurs in college corridors through teasing or physical intimidation, but cyberbullying is far more insidious as it invades the safety of our homes. With the widespread availability of smartphones among students my age, social media platforms have become hotspots for harassment. Anonymous trolls or even classmates can spread rumors, share private photos, or leave hateful comments that destroy a student’s self-esteem. The consequences are alarming; victims often suffer from depression, anxiety, and a drastic drop in academic performance. Some even withdraw from social interactions entirely, fearing judgment. Unlike physical bullying, digital harassment leaves a permanent footprint and can go viral instantly, making the humiliation feel inescapable. To combat this, strict laws and school policies are necessary, but family and peer support are equally vital. We must cultivate a culture of empathy where bystanders speak up rather than scrolling past. As students, we must realize that our online behavior has real-world consequences. Mental health counseling should be prioritized in colleges to support victims, ensuring that no student feels isolated or helpless in the face of such aggression.

3. Price Hike

Price hike, or the persistent increase in the cost of essential commodities, is currently one of the most agonizing problems for the common people, including students and their families. In recent times, the market prices of daily necessities like rice, oil, eggs, and stationeries have skyrocketed, often going beyond the purchasing power of the middle and lower classes. For a student, this economic instability has a direct impact on education. Many parents struggle to pay tuition fees, buy books, or afford transportation costs, forcing some students to compromise on their nutrition or even drop out to support their families. The causes are multifaceted, ranging from global inflation and fuel price surges to the manipulation of local markets by dishonest syndicates who hoard goods to create artificial crises. While the wealthy remain unaffected, the fixed-income group suffers the most, leading to a decline in the standard of living. To mitigate this, the government must strictly monitor markets and break the monopoly of syndicates. Additionally, social safety nets for low-income families should be expanded. As citizens, we must also practice austerity. However, without strict administrative control and systemic economic reforms, the burden of price hikes will continue to crush the dreams of many aspiring students.

4. Climate Change/ Global Warming

Climate change is an existential threat that is particularly alarming for low-lying delta lands like ours. It refers to the long-term shift in global weather patterns, primarily driven by Global Warming—the rising average temperature of the Earth due to greenhouse gas emissions. For a student observing the changing seasons, the effects are undeniable. We now experience erratic rainfall, blistering heatwaves during summer, and milder winters, all of which disrupt agriculture and daily life. The melting of polar ice caps leads to a rise in sea levels, which threatens to submerge the southern coastal districts of our country, potentially creating millions of climate refugees. This environmental crisis is largely man-made, resulting from deforestation, rapid industrialization, and the burning of fossil fuels. The consequences extend to health hazards, as rising temperatures breed vector-borne diseases like dengue. Combating this requires immediate global and local action. While international agreements are crucial, we must also act locally by planting more trees, reducing plastic use, and conserving energy. Youth activism is vital here; we must raise awareness and demand sustainable policies. If we fail to act now, the next generation will inherit a planet that is hostile and perhaps uninhabitable, making climate action our moral obligation.

5. City Life and Rural Life

City life and rural life present two contrasting pictures of society, each with its unique set of advantages and challenges. For a student in a metropolis, life is fast-paced and filled with opportunities. Cities offer better educational institutions, advanced medical facilities, and diverse career prospects. However, this comes at a cost; city dwellers constantly battle severe traffic jams, air pollution, and a lack of open spaces, leading to a stressful and mechanical existence. On the other hand, rural life is characterized by tranquility, greenery, and a strong sense of community. The air is fresh, and food is often free from dangerous adulteration. People in villages share a bond of brotherhood that is often missing in the concrete jungle of cities. Yet, rural areas frequently suffer from a lack of modern amenities, quality colleges, and reliable healthcare, forcing many to migrate to cities. While city life drives the economy and modernization, rural life sustains the country's agricultural backbone and cultural heritage. Ideally, the gap between the two should be bridged. By decentralizing development and ensuring urban facilities in village areas, we can enjoy the serenity of rural life without compromising on modern necessities, creating a balanced environment for future generations.Online education has emerged as a revolutionary alternative to traditional schooling, especially gaining prominence during the recent global pandemic. It refers to a system where students learn remotely using the internet and digital devices like smartphones or laptops. For students in our country, this mode of learning has been a mixed bag. On the positive side, it ensures that education continues during crises, such as political instability or natural disasters, preventing academic session jams. It also allows access to global resources, enabling us to learn from international platforms like Coursera or YouTube. It offers flexibility, saving time and energy spent on commuting through traffic. However, significant challenges remain. A large portion of the student population lacks stable internet connections or affordable devices, creating a digital divide between urban and rural learners. Furthermore, online classes often lack the discipline and direct teacher-student interaction found in physical classrooms, leading to reduced concentration. Prolonged screen time also causes health issues like eye strain. To make online education truly effective, authorities must ensure affordable data and universal internet access. While it cannot fully replace the social environment of a campus, a blended approach of online and offline learning is likely the future of education.

7. July Uprising/ 36th July

The July Uprising of 2024, often metaphorically referred to as the "36th July," marks a watershed moment in our national history, driven entirely by the power of students and the masses. What began as a demand for logical quota reform in government jobs rapidly transformed into a one-point movement for the resignation of an authoritarian regime. The term "36th July" symbolizes the extension of the struggle beyond the month of July, culminating in the victory on August 5th. For a student of my generation, this uprising was not just political; it was a fight for dignity, freedom of speech, and justice against years of corruption and oppression. Students stood fearlessly before bullets, creating a wall of resistance that eventually toppled the government. The walls of our cities, now painted with vibrant graffiti, stand as testaments to this revolution. However, the cost was high, with hundreds of students and civilians martyred and thousands injured. This uprising proved that the youth are the true architects of the nation. It has burdened us with the responsibility of rebuilding a renewed nation where democracy and meritocracy prevail. We must now work to uphold the spirit of this sacrifice and ensure that discrimination never takes root again.

8. Food Adulteration

Food adulteration has become a silent killer in our society, posing a grave threat to public health and the physical well-being of the younger generation. It involves the unethical practice of adding harmful chemicals or low-quality substances to food items to increase quantity or enhance appearance. In our markets, it is terrifyingly common to find formaldehyde in fish to keep it fresh, calcium carbide in fruits to ripen them quickly, and textile dyes in sweets and confectioneries. Even daily essentials like milk and oil are not spared from contamination. For a student, consuming such adulterated food leads to immediate issues like food poisoning and long-term fatal diseases such as cancer, kidney failure, and heart defects. This greed-driven crime is destroying the nation's vitality, as a sick generation cannot contribute to development. Although there are laws like the Food Safety Act, their implementation is often weak due to corruption and lack of monitoring. Mobile courts occasionally fine offenders, but this is not enough. We need strict surveillance at every stage of the food supply chain and severe punishment for perpetrators. Moreover, consumer awareness is vital; we must demand safe food as a basic human right to secure a healthy future for ourselves.

9. Impacts of Facebook/ Social Media

Social media, particularly Facebook, has fundamentally altered how we communicate, offering both immense benefits and serious drawbacks for students. On the positive side, it serves as a powerful tool for connectivity and information. We use Messenger groups to share class notes, discuss assignments, and stay updated on current affairs. It also provides a platform for social awareness, where students can raise their voices against injustice. However, the abuses of social media are alarming. It is incredibly addictive; many students spend hours scrolling through newsfeeds, killing valuable study time and disrupting sleep cycles. This addiction often leads to a lack of concentration and poor academic results. Furthermore, the spread of fake news and rumors on Facebook can incite violence and social unrest. There is also the psychological impact; seeing the curated "perfect" lives of others can trigger feelings of inadequacy and depression among teenagers. Cyberbullying and privacy breaches are additional risks that jeopardize our safety. Therefore, social media is a double-edged sword. To maximize its benefits, we must practice digital discipline, limiting our screen time and verifying information before sharing. It should be used as a tool for empowerment and learning, not as a master that controls our lives.

10. Human Rights

Human rights are the fundamental rights and freedoms that belong to every person in the world, from birth until death, regardless of their race, religion, gender, or status. These include the right to life, freedom of speech, education, and protection from torture. For a student, understanding human rights is crucial because it forms the basis of a just and democratic society. In many parts of the world, including developing nations, violations of these rights are frequent. We see children forced into labor instead of going to school, women denied equal opportunities, and citizens silenced for criticizing authorities. The recent student movements in our country have also highlighted the importance of the right to protest and freedom of assembly. When human rights are upheld, society flourishes with peace and dignity. However, when they are ignored, it leads to oppression and instability. International bodies like the UN and local human rights organizations work to protect these liberties, but true enforcement begins with awareness. As the future leaders of the nation, we must be educated about our rights and the rights of others. We must stand against discrimination and injustice, ensuring that the principles of equality and liberty are not just words in a constitution but a reality for everyone.

11. Adolescence

Adolescence is a critical transition period between childhood and adulthood, roughly encompassing the teenage years. It is a time of rapid physical, psychological, and social changes that shape a person’s future identity. For a student in Class XII, this is the current reality. We experience a surge of emotions, a desire for independence, and the pressure to fit in with peers. While it is a time of immense energy and potential, it is also a phase of vulnerability. Adolescents often face confusion regarding their career paths and personal relationships. In our social context, many teenagers lack proper guidance and are hesitant to discuss their physical and mental changes with parents due to conservative taboos. This communication gap can lead to risky behaviors, such as drug abuse or internet addiction. Furthermore, academic pressure during this time is immense, often leading to stress and anxiety. To navigate this turbulence, a supportive environment is essential. Parents and teachers need to be more approachable and empathetic, treating adolescents as friends rather than subordinates. Proper counseling and open dialogue can help us channel our youthful energy into creativity and leadership, turning this challenging phase into the most productive foundation for our adult lives.

12. Civic Engagement

Civic engagement refers to the active participation of individuals in the life of their community to improve conditions for others and shape the community’s future. For students, this goes beyond just voting; it involves volunteering, environmental activism, and participating in local governance or social reforms. In our country, the youth have historically played a pivotal role in civic duties, from language movements to disaster relief efforts during floods. Engaging in such activities teaches us responsibility, empathy, and leadership skills that textbooks cannot provide. For instance, when students manage traffic during crises or clean up neighborhoods, they are practicing civic engagement. It strengthens the democratic fabric of a nation by ensuring that citizens are not just passive observers but active contributors. However, apathy is a growing challenge; many young people are becoming self-centered or discouraged by dirty politics. To counter this, educational institutions should encourage community service as part of the curriculum. When we engage civically, we realize that the government alone cannot solve every problem. By working together to address issues like waste management, literacy, or public safety, we build a more resilient and inclusive society. Civic engagement is the heartbeat of a functioning democracy.

13. Environmental Justice

Environmental justice is the principle that all people, regardless of their race, income, or social class, have a right to a clean, healthy, and sustainable environment. It argues that no specific group should bear a disproportionate share of negative environmental consequences. In the context of our country, this is a highly relevant issue. Often, factories and brick kilns are set up near poor rural villages or urban slums, polluting the air and water of those who have the least political power to object. These marginalized communities suffer from respiratory diseases and contaminated water, while the factory owners, living in posh areas, remain unaffected. This inequality is a violation of basic human rights. Environmental justice demands that these vulnerable groups have a voice in decision-making processes regarding land use and industrial projects. For students, understanding this concept is vital to becoming responsible citizens. We must advocate for policies that hold polluters accountable and ensure that development does not come at the cost of the poor. True development is inclusive; it protects the river for the fisherman just as it provides electricity for the city. Achieving environmental justice requires legal reforms, corporate responsibility, and active public awareness to ensure a fair and green future for all.

14. Water/Environment Pollution

Environment pollution, particularly water pollution, is a catastrophic issue threatening the biodiversity and public health of our riverine nation. Water pollution occurs when harmful substances like industrial waste, chemicals, and sewage are discharged into water bodies without treatment. As a student, I witness the dying state of rivers near major cities; the water is pitch black and foul-smelling, unable to sustain aquatic life. The primary culprits are unplanned industrialization, the dumping of plastic waste, and the lack of proper sewage systems. Farmers also contribute by using excessive chemical fertilizers that wash into ponds and rivers. The impact is devastating: waterborne diseases like cholera and typhoid are rampant, and the scarcity of clean drinking water is becoming acute. Beyond water, air and soil pollution are also choking our environment. The burning of fossil fuels and brick kilns fills the air with toxic dust. To save our environment, immediate and strict implementation of environmental laws is necessary. Industries must be compelled to use Effluent Treatment Plants (ETP). Furthermore, we must stop the culture of treating rivers as garbage dumps. Mass awareness campaigns and student-led cleanup drives can make a difference. We must remember that nature is not a resource to be exploited endlessly, but a home that must be protected.

15. The Greta Effect

The "Greta Effect" refers to the global phenomenon of increased climate awareness and activism sparked by the young Swedish activist Greta Thunberg. Starting with her solitary "School Strike for Climate," she inspired millions of students worldwide to demand urgent action from world leaders regarding the climate crisis. For a student in a climate-vulnerable country like ours, the Greta Effect is deeply inspiring. It has taught us that age is not a barrier to making a difference and that one voice can ignite a global movement. Motivated by her example, many youth groups in our country have formed to protect rivers, stop deforestation, and advocate for sustainable policies. This effect has shifted the narrative; climate change is no longer just a scientific debate but a moral emergency. It pressures governments and corporations to be more transparent about their carbon footprints. However, awareness alone is not enough. The Greta Effect must translate into tangible actions, such as reducing plastic use, planting trees, and shifting towards renewable energy. It serves as a wake-up call for my generation to take ownership of our future. It reminds us that we are the last generation that can prevent the worst effects of climate change, and we must act now.
